Bit.krshr

a DIY effect pedal on an Arduino Nano-compatible board
(ELEGOO Nano V3, ATmega328P)

A lo-fi bit crusher for electric guitar, built on a breadboard and programmed in C++.
I started it to learn C/C++ with a project I actually want to play.

Demo and prototype

What it does

The guitar signal is read by the Nano's analog-to-digital converter, degraded in software, and sent back out as PWM. Two knobs shape the sound:

  • Bit depth (pin A1): from 8 bits down to 1 bit. Fewer bits mean a rougher, more distorted signal.
  • Sample rate (pin A2): how many times per second the signal is measured. Fewer measurements make the sound muffled and metallic.

Signal path

  1. Input: the guitar tip goes through a 104 (100 nF) capacitor. Two 1 MΩ resistors hold the signal at about 2.5 V, so the Nano can read the negative half of the waveform.
  2. Read: analogRead(A0) returns a number from 0 to 1023. The code estimates the DC level and subtracts it, which leaves only the audio.
  3. Crush: a sample-and-hold lowers the sample rate, and bit shifting lowers the bit depth.
  4. Output: Timer2 generates fast PWM at 62.5 kHz on pin D3. A 330 Ω resistor and a 104 capacitor smooth it, and a 10 µF capacitor blocks the DC before the speaker jack.

Parts

  • Arduino Nano-compatible board (ELEGOO Nano V3, ATmega328P)
  • Breadboard and jumper wires
  • 2 × 6.3 mm jack sockets (input and output)
  • 2 × 1 MΩ resistors (bias)
  • 1 × 330 Ω resistor (output filter)
  • 2 × 104 ceramic capacitors (input coupling, output filter)
  • 1 × 10 µF electrolytic capacitor (output DC blocking)
  • 2 × trimpots (bit depth, sample rate)

What I learned

  • C and C++ types have fixed sizes: on the Nano an int is only 16 bits, and overflows fail silently.
  • Bitwise operators (&, |, <<, >>) and how to set hardware registers with them.
  • The difference between DC and AC, and why a guitar signal needs a bias before an ADC can read it.
  • Debugging hardware: floating pins, a reversed electrolytic capacitor, and loose breadboard contacts.

Next steps

Replace the trimpots with panel potentiometers, add a footswitch, solder the circuit, and put it in a pedal enclosure. A second effect on another chip is planned.
